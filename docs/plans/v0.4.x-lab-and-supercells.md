# Plan: v0.4.x — The Lab and supercells

- **Status:** Complete: v0.4.0 to v0.4.9 shipped (sections 12 and 13)
- **Date:** 2026-10-22
- **Review:** ChatGPT-6 Sol, 2026-10-22. The review keeps anomaly detection outside this roadmap and narrows evidence claims to what Quantarium can verify.
- **Scope:** A Lab where strategy ideas are tested under rules that stop them from flattering their authors, and supercells: cells whose decisions come from a trained model, a language model, or both. The series also gives contributors a tracked place for this work, starting with the ML research on the `ml-signals` branch ([PR #2](https://github.com/f1ymango/quantarium/pull/2)).

## 1. Goal

Every backtester can report that a strategy made +500%. Few can say whether to believe it. Quantarium's aim for v0.4.x is that **every result carries an evidence status with its scope and caveats, and Quantarium enforces the conditions it can observe**:

- Results show whether their recorded information boundary precedes the evaluated sessions. A declared boundary is not treated as proof until it is bound to a verifiable model training record.
- An idea is judged by rules fixed before its results are seen.
- Every result is compared with simple controls.
- The number of attempts Quantarium observed is counted and reported, with an explicit warning that offline attempts are unknown.
- Results that cannot be trusted say why.

Simple and Complex continue to describe a cell's portfolio structure. A model-driven decision source is an independent capability; it does not create a third portfolio mode. A supercell is a presentation of a model-assisted cell and its evidence, never a claim of superior returns. Its card shows the scope and strength of that evidence, not only returns.

## 2. Lessons from the `ml-signals` research

The ML work in PR #2 (ADR-0023 to ADR-0029 on that branch) is the starting evidence for this plan:

- Price and volume features predicted the size of moves better than chance, but not their direction.
- A risk model for large drops was accurate and calibrated, yet three ways of acting on it did not improve return per unit of risk: daily stops with re-entry, monthly risk-scaled sizing, and cutting exposure in risky months.
- Insider purchases were not supported as a signal by a pre-registered test.
- Monthly equal-weight rebalancing was supported, narrowly, with a survivor-bias caveat that may account for all of it.
- The method mattered more than the models: walk-forward training, labels timed to Quantarium's execution, pass rules committed before each test, automatic controls, and honest verdicts.

v0.4.x builds that method into Quantarium so that it applies to every user and every agent, not only to careful ones.

## 3. Mechanisms

### 3.1 Time locks, enforced by the engine

A result is worthless if the strategy already knew the outcome. Quantarium records the latest outcome date disclosed by the model's training record and labels sessions against it. The first v0.4.0 field is self-declared, so it is recorded as unverified and cannot establish admissibility (ADR-0030). Model-card binding and verification follow in v0.4.2:

- **ML supercells** carry `information_end`: the last date any training label used. Labels look ahead (a five-session label for 28 December ends in January), so `information_end` is later than the last training date. Sessions on or before it can run, but are marked **not admissible** and never count toward a verdict.
- **LLM supercells** record a provider-disclosed training cutoff and a pinned model identifier when available. Real-data sessions on or before that cutoff are **contaminated**. A missing, uncertain, or changed cutoff yields **unknown**, not a clean historical verdict. Pinning an identifier does not prove what training data it contained.
- **Label timing.** A strategy decides after the close and fills at the next open, as the engine does today. Features may not read the bar they trade on.

Once model cards exist, the boundary belongs to the model's training record rather than to a date typed from memory. Retraining with later labels must move it forward. Quantarium cannot infer an undisclosed provider's exact training data from a model name or cutoff statement.

### 3.2 Testing language models without their memory

A language model trained after a period may remember what happened in it, so a backtest on that period can measure recall as well as decision quality. Quantarium offers three ways to probe that risk, with different evidential strength:

- **Synthetic scenarios:** generated markets with made-up symbols. Exact synthetic paths created after a model was trained are unlikely to have been memorized, but scenario design can still favor a strategy and does not establish real-market performance.
- **The disguised replay:** the same real history with symbols renamed, dates shifted, and prices rescaled. The Lab reports any performance gap as a diagnostic for sensitivity to identity and presentation. Those transformations may also change legitimate strategy inputs, so the gap cannot establish that the model recalled an outcome. Disguised historical results never earn a clean verdict on their own.
- **Forward tests:** Feed experiments on data that arrives after the model's disclosed cutoff and after the strategy and test rules were frozen. This is the strongest of the three for assessing behavior on real data, subject to provider and data provenance limits.

### 3.3 The tape

Every answer a language model gives a strategy is recorded: the prompt's hash, the pinned model version, the answer, and the cost.

- **Reproducibility.** A replay reads the tape instead of calling the model, so the same input still gives the same result, at no cost.
- **Audit.** Each decision can be traced to the answer behind it.
- **Comparison.** Another model can be asked the same recorded prompts, so two models are compared on identical inputs.

### 3.4 Controls, ablation, and attempts

- **Controls:** every experiment includes buy-and-hold and equal-weight cells with the same starting holdings.
- **Ablation:** a hybrid supercell is also run with each part replaced (the language model by "hold", the ML part by nothing), so the Lab shows what each part adds.
- **Attempts:** the workspace counts variants it observes for each idea and reports that count. A deflated Sharpe ratio is shown only when the Lab has the underlying trial results, an effective trial count, enough observations, and return-distribution inputs required by the method. Otherwise it shows the observed count and an explicit limitation. Offline or renamed trials cannot be counted reliably.

### 3.5 Pre-registration and used-up data

- An experiment's cells, scenarios, measure, and pass rule are frozen and hashed before it runs.
- Data that judged an idea is marked used for that idea. Variants of it can still run there, but cannot earn a verdict.
- A verdict is *supported within the tested scope*, *not supported*, *inconclusive*, or *unknown*. Every verdict names its period, universe, data provenance, costs, controls, and information boundary. Missing point-in-time membership, model cutoffs, or trial history can prevent a broader claim. User-visible results may still be explored after their data are used up, but cannot earn a fresh verdict on that same data.
- The **ledger** keeps every experiment, including failures.

### 3.6 Agents in the Lab

- Agents read the ledger through `quantarium mcp`, so they know what was already tried and what was found.
- An agent proposes an experiment; the user approves its pre-registration in the Inbox (ADR-0017).
- Holdout scenarios (ADR-0018) stay hidden, and used-up data stops an agent from searching until something passes.

### 3.7 The `ask` gateway

Strategies never reach a model directly. In a script, `context.ask(prompt, schema)` sends a request over the sandbox's existing pipe. Quantarium then:

1. checks the cell's budget, its decision rhythm, the cache, and the tape;
2. calls the model through one of the user's connections (ADR-0021, ADR-0022), with the usual logging and redaction;
3. checks the answer against the schema (weights, a choice such as hold or trim, a confidence) and returns only that.

The model gets no tools. Scripts get no network access and no keys. Answers are untrusted data, and the injection fixtures from v0.3.0 are extended to cover them.

## 4. Requirements from known traps

| Trap | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| A Lab that allows unlimited tweaking turns into a machine for false discoveries | Pre-registration, used-up data, and attempt counts (3.4, 3.5) |
| Backtests of language models on history they remember look spectacular and fail live | Contamination labels, disguised replay, synthetic scenarios, forward tests (3.1, 3.2) |
| Scripts calling models themselves need network access and keys, which breaks the sandbox | The `ask` gateway is the only path to a model (3.7) |
| Model answers vary, which breaks "same input, same result" and Feed resumes | The tape (3.3) |
| Thousands of calls per backtest, multiplied by suites, variants, and agents | Budgets in calls, tokens, and cost; a decision rhythm; the cache; the user's review before a script that uses `ask` runs |
| Universes of today's index members flatter strategies that buy losers | Survivor-bias warnings; point-in-time universes where data allows |
| A provider changes or retires a model, and old verdicts still look valid | Verdicts pinned to a model version and marked stale when it changes |
| Text in market data steers a model's decisions | Answers limited to the schema; inputs treated as data; injection fixtures |
| "Supercell" suggests better returns, and a language model suggests advice | Cards show evidence levels; Quantarium never presents a supercell as a recommendation |
| Heavy ML libraries in the core; pickled models execute code when loaded | ML stays in the optional `[ml]` extra; training runs in a separate process; models are stored as data |
| Language-model latency does not fit minute bars | `ask` runs on daily or slower rhythms only; Live supercells use stored models |

## 8. As-built security maintenance (2026-10-23)

The post-incident repository review found no LiteLLM dependency or known startup-file indicator in `main` or the project virtual environment. The existing `setuptools`, `tzdata`, and `keyring` requirements lacked upper bounds; they now have them. `tests/test_repository_policy.py` checks the dependency allowlist and bounds, and rejects workflows that reintroduce unpinned external actions, `pull_request_target`, missing permissions, or pip installs without hashes. It failed on the three requirements before they were fixed.

The expectations workflow remains only on the unmerged `ml-review` and `ml-signals` branches. Its tagged actions and unresolved `pip install -e ".[ml]"` fail the new policy. The workflow is still subject to maintainer review and approval if anyone proposes bringing it back.

## 9. Review of v0.4.0 to v0.4.2 (2026-10-23)

Claude reviewed the v0.4.0 to v0.4.2 work against the security invariants in `AGENTS.md` and the Lab's purpose. Working probes ran as a hostile agent with every tier.

**Held.**

- Agents cannot register or run Lab experiments. Their Lab proposals cannot name holdout scenarios, hidden paths, or paths outside the workspace.
- Experiments that use a scenario later marked as holdout disappear from agents' ledger, experiment reads, and cell cards.
- Model cards are data only, content-addressed, and loaded only from a models folder.
- Unreviewed agent code in the sandbox still returns weights only.
- The Lab page has no raw-HTML sinks.
- The order engine's tests cover gaps, stop-before-limit, partial fills, expiry, checkpoints, and splits.

**Fixed in v0.4.3.**

- A tie with the best control passed the default rule (`>=` against a threshold of 0). A copy of buy-and-hold was "supported" with an edge of 0.00.
- A failed candidate's reason was dropped between the journal and the verdict.
- Agents could store hidden paths inside a cell spec. They were never read, but they broke the path rule.
- Simple cells returning a dict got a Python type error.

**Not changed.**

- The `ask` gateway work-in-progress branch (`codex/v0.4.3-ask-wip`) is unmerged. It becomes the starting point for v0.4.4 after its own review.
- The Lab ledger includes local absolute paths (strategy root, model card) in records agents can read. The workspace overview already exposes the root, so this adds nothing new.
